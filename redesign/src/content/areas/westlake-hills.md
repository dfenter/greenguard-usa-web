Westlake Hills has some of the highest property values in the Austin metro and some of the highest mosquito pressure per acre. The combination of Barton Creek proximity, dense live oak canopy, and mature irrigated landscapes creates an interconnected web of tiny breeding sites that sustains tiger mosquito colonies regardless of rainfall.

## Westlake Hills Mosquito Hotspots

**Barton Creek Greenbelt corridor** properties in lower Westlake Hills face the highest sustained pressure. Creek-side lots experience both container-breeding tiger mosquitoes and the larger Culex species from the creek itself.

**Davenport Ranch** properties with pool equipment, water features, and dense oak canopy combine to create premium tiger mosquito habitat. Shaded terraced yards common here are particularly high-risk.

**Rob Roy** estate properties have the acreage and canopy to sustain large populations. Ornamental ponds, creek-adjacent lots, and extensive irrigation infrastructure are common pressure drivers.

**Rollingwood** older homes with mature vegetation and established container plants often have decades of accumulated breeding micro-habitat that sustains pressure through dry spells.

## Westlake Hills Mosquito Season

Season runs approximately March 15 through November 1. The watershed's consistent moisture supply extends the active period on both ends. Tiger mosquitoes overwinter successfully here and begin biting as early as late February in warm years.
