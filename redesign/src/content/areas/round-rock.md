Round Rock homeowners deal with both the Asian tiger mosquito (*Aedes albopictus*) and southern house mosquito (*Culex quinquefasciatus*). Tiger mosquitoes bite aggressively during daylight in shaded yards; house mosquitoes create problems at dusk near standing water. CO₂ trapping addresses both species effectively, unlike spray programs designed for a single species.

## Round Rock Mosquito Hotspots

**Old Settlers Park area** and adjacent neighborhoods experience the highest pressure in Round Rock from the park's irrigation runoff, creek tributary corridors, and sports complex drainage.

**Brushy Creek corridor** through north Round Rock connects to Cedar Park's high-pressure zones, creating a continuous mosquito corridor along the trail system.

**Teravista and Mayfield Ranch** neighborhoods have retention pond infrastructure that sustains mosquito populations through dry periods when natural breeding sites dry up.

**Round Rock West and Highlands** near McNeil Road see persistent pressure from creek-adjacent lots and mature landscape with established irrigation.

## Round Rock Mosquito Season

Round Rock's season runs March through November. The city averages 233 frost-free days annually, one of the longest windows in Williamson County, meaning mosquito populations build early in spring and persist well into fall.
