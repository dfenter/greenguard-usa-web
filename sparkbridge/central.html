SparkBridge Central

One server in the middle, holding your whole plant network together

Every site's Edge gateway sends its data to one place. Central is that place. It collects what every site reports and gives your analysts, your auditors and your own team one version of what is happening, instead of forty. SparkCalc and SparkID are included. Optional modules build on top of it: Passage, Sentinel, FleetOps, SparkVault, SparkInject and GitOps / Enterprise Governance. Each one runs only on a SparkBridge Central gateway. Provider, the Sparkplug-aware broker, is $1,995, its own license, and works with Edge and Central or with any conformant Sparkplug host.

One Rate, One Total, One Efficiency Number, Calculated Once

The same formula for a rate, a total or an efficiency gets built three times: once in a PLC that's expensive to change, once in a dashboard nobody else can see, once in a spreadsheet that quietly drifts out of sync with the other two.

SparkCalc computes it once, in one place, on live plant data, and every system that needs the number uses the same one. Change the formula once and every consumer picks it up.

What it replaces: the same rate, total or efficiency formula built three times over, in a PLC, a dashboard and a spreadsheet, each one able to drift out of sync with the others.

What you get in the first month: a rate, a total and an efficiency number computed once against live data, timestamped correctly even when its inputs arrive at different times, with the same number showing up everywhere it's used.

A Read Permission Never Quietly Includes Control

Access rules for a plant data network are usually hand-typed. A wildcard written to grant read access can silently grant control too, and nobody notices until an audit or an incident finds it. Credentials shared between two roles can't be rotated or revoked one at a time.

SparkID generates access rules from a plain statement of intent, so a permission does exactly what you meant and nothing more: who can read, who can send commands, safe rotation of credentials, without a human ever typing a wildcard.

What it replaces: hand-typed access wildcards and shared credentials that can't be rotated or revoked one identity at a time.

What you get in the first month: your plant network's access rules regenerated from a plain statement of who should read what and who should control what, with a dry-run diff before anything changes and rotation that never drops a live session.

What you get in the first month: a scored report of your plant data network, ranked by severity, with the evidence behind each finding and the concrete fix, run first against your own estate with zero false criticals.

History That Stays True Even After an Outage

Most systems record when a value arrives, not when it happened. After an outage, a real production curve becomes a flat wall of nothing, then a jump, and every report built on it is wrong.

SparkVault keeps your plant's history on the plant's own clock, in an open database your existing tools can read directly. An outage never leaves a hole, and any past day can be replayed on demand.

What it replaces: per-site historians and manual data exports, and the flat trend and wrong shift report a real outage used to leave behind.

What you get in the first month: your plant network's history in one place, on the time it actually happened, read directly by your own reporting and dashboard tools, with any past day available to replay on demand.

Your Plant Data in Your Warehouse, With No Meter Running

Getting production data into a warehouse is normally a per-row subscription and a standing worry about whether a reading was lost or counted twice during a restart.

SparkInject delivers your production data into your own warehouse continuously, once, with every reading tracked so a restart or a dropped link can't cause a gap or a duplicate. You own the account and the bill. We're never in the middle of it. Snowflake is the first connector family. Databricks and BigQuery follow.

What it replaces: per-row cloud connector subscriptions and nightly exports, and the standing worry about a reading lost or counted twice during a restart.

What you get in the first month: a continuous feed from plant to warehouse, landing in your own Snowflake account on the time the plant recorded it, with nothing missing and nothing duplicated across a dropped link.
