When a connector resends a backlog it usually redelivers readings that already landed, so someone spends the morning de-duplicating before anyone can run a query. A nightly batch that is always a day behind the decision
You're asked about this morning's production while holding yesterday's export.		 A batch can only answer questions about a day that has already closed, and cycle times or downtime read from it reflect when the export ran, not when the plant ran. SparkInject streams straight into your own Snowflake account continuously, and each reading keeps the time the plant recorded it, so timing analysis matches the floor.
Paying per row to move your own data
You watch a connector bill grow with production volume.		 A per-row or per-connector subscription means the more you produce, the more it costs to look at what you produced, and a vendor sits permanently in the data path. SparkInject is bought once per gateway and writes into the Snowflake account you already own and already pay for. We're never in the middle of it, and the cost doesn't move when the plant runs harder.
