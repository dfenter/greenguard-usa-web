Three Situations This Solves

Three places where the history you already collect stops being usable, and what changes.

The situationWhat changes
A four hour outage in the middle of a reported shift
Any site whose link to the central system is not perfect.		 Most systems record when a value arrived, so the trend goes flat for the outage then jumps at reconnect. The shift report built on that curve is wrong, and you cannot defend it to a customer or a regulator. SparkVault stores every value on the timestamp the equipment gave it, so the backlog fills in where it actually belongs. In a four hour and eleven minute disconnection every reading came back on its real time and nothing was lost.
History locked in a file your reporting tools cannot read
Plants running a separate historian per site, each in its own proprietary format.		 Getting a number out means an export, a ticket, or a negotiation for access to your own data, and every site answers the question differently. SparkVault keeps history for the whole plant network in one open database on your own gateway, so the trends and reports your team already uses read it directly.
Tuning an alarm or walking an incident with no real data
Engineers reviewing what happened, or testing a dashboard before it goes live.		 A simulated signal never has the noise, the dropouts or the timing of the real plant, so a threshold tuned against it behaves differently in service. SparkVault plays any past day back on demand through your systems, at the resolution the plant wrote. You can re-run the hour an incident happened, test a new dashboard, or set an alarm against production data instead of a guess.

SparkVault suits plants that already have data flowing and now need it kept properly: multiple sites, intermittent links, reporting that has to hold up under scrutiny. It installs alongside what you have and changes nothing upstream.

For your engineers

SparkVault is a Sparkplug historian module for Ignition. It records every value with the timestamp the equipment gave it, not the moment it reached the broker, and stores that history in an open database rather than a proprietary file so your existing reporting and dashboard tools can read it directly. Any past day replays on demand. SparkFlow, licensed on its own, composes its own transport-tier replay with SparkVault's store-tier replay for one operator incident workflow. Underneath, it runs on the SparkBridge Host and Edge described on the technology page; measured figures are in Performance Quantified and the feature comparison.
