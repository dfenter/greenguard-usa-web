SparkCalc

The numbers people actually report on

Nobody reports raw sensor readings. They report efficiency, throughput, uptime and cost per unit. Those numbers are usually calculated in a spreadsheet, a dashboard and a PLC, each giving a slightly different answer.

One number, calculated once, used everywhere.

SparkCalc computes derived values centrally and publishes them back as first-class plant data. Every dashboard, report and analyst then reads the same number, because there is only one.

Each value declares what it was calculated from, so when someone asks where a figure came from the answer is in the system rather than in someone's memory.

It can also recalculate over history. Define a new metric today and get it for the last six months, computed the same way, rather than waiting half a year to start trending it.

Four ways a reported number goes wrong before anyone reads it, and what changes.

The same efficiency built three times, giving three answers
Anyone whose PLC, dashboard and spreadsheet disagree in a Monday meeting.		 Each copy of the formula was written by a different person at a different time, and they drift. The meeting turns into an argument about which number is right rather than what to do about it. SparkCalc computes the value once, centrally, and publishes it back as first-class plant data. A rate or a total nobody can trace back to its inputs
Engineers asked to defend a figure that has already left the building.		 When the derivation lives in a spreadsheet cell or in somebody's memory, there is no way to show how the figure was produced. Every SparkCalc value declares what it was calculated from and carries the timestamp of its oldest input, so the derivation sits in the system. Nearly twelve hours of recomputed history, 4,201 values, was checked against an independent implementation and agreed on every value, timestamp and quality flag.
A derived number that stays confident while one of its inputs is dead
Reports built on a KPI that quietly went stale during a dropout.		 Most calculations keep publishing on the last value they saw, so the KPI looks fine while the sensor feeding it is offline, and nobody finds out until the month closes. SparkCalc marks a derived value bad the moment any input goes bad. In a window containing 17 real equipment dropouts the derived series went bad across every one of them and never held a stale number.
A new metric you would otherwise wait six months to trend
Teams defining a KPI today and needing last season's version of it.		 A metric normally starts accumulating on the day it is defined, so any comparison waits for the calendar. SparkCalc recalculates over history, so a metric defined today can be produced for the last six months, computed exactly the same way as the live one.

SparkCalc suits teams whose KPIs are recalculated in several places and do not agree, or who need a defensible answer to how a reported number was produced.
