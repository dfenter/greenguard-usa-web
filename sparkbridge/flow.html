SparkFlow

Sparkplug into NATS, kept honest

Most bridges into a streaming backbone drop the industrial data model at the door: what is left is opaque payloads on a subject, with no idea whether a value is fresh, stale or missing entirely. SparkFlow carries Sparkplug's birth, death, quality and source timestamp across the seam, so the backbone can answer questions your equipment already answered once.

Sparkplug stays the contract. NATS becomes the tier north of it.

SparkFlow does not replace the broker. Sparkplug-over-MQTT stays the OT contract at the edge, exactly as it is today, with the same Edge, Host and Provider modules and the same TCK-certified codec. SparkFlow is what sits north of that: a certified translation that decodes Sparkplug once and republishes it as NATS subjects and a JetStream stream, carrying the semantics the wire format leaves opaque otherwise, birth and death, quality, stale, and the timestamp the equipment actually gave the reading, not the moment it arrived.

The payoff is the twelve-million-point problem. Most of a plant's points are historized and almost none are alarmed or ever rendered on a screen at once, so most of them do not need to live as a tag object. SparkFlow's KV store holds a last-known value with quality per metric with no tag behind it; only the subset a site actually alarms or views gets projected into an Ignition tag through the existing Host managed-provider path. Everything else is still there, still queryable, still carrying its real timestamp.

JetStream also makes the transport itself durable and replayable, not just the store behind it. A consumer that joins late, drops, or needs to re-walk a window can resume or replay at the transport tier, on top of the store-tier replay SparkVault already does. Command write-back is request/reply over NATS, and it is deliberately fail-closed: without configured NATS credentials and TLS, the command bridge does not start at all, telemetry publish is unaffected either way.

What Is Proven, What Is Owed

SparkFlow is commissioning, not released. This is the actual state, not the pitch.

Proven

  • Sparkplug decode reuses the same certified codec as Edge and Host, no reimplementation.
  • Birth/death, quality, stale and source timestamp verified end to end against a live, licensed Ignition gateway.
  • Metric names with spaces and other subject-illegal characters escape reversibly instead of failing silently, closing a real gap found in live verification.
  • Leaf-node store-and-forward proven with a real 60-second network partition on a second, genuinely disconnected box: 447/447 reconciled, zero loss.
  • Command bridge verified fail-closed without configured credentials and TLS.
  • Restart, redelivery and KV-compaction failure scenarios pass their gates on the transport-tier bench harness.

Owed

  • Full-scale throughput against the twelve-million-point hypothesis has not reached target on a contended bench box; the transport itself drops nothing, but the harness's single consumer is the bottleneck at scale.
  • Provider tags confirmed live over the token-gated /tags route: seven TAGFEED-projected tags returned with real values and quality.
  • Exactly-once identity is live: every published metric carries a Nats-Msg-Id built from a per-session UUID and a non-wrapping sequence, verified on the wire past the 256-message Sparkplug wraparound.
  • Native cross-domain JetStream stream sourcing across a leaf boundary needs an account export/import fix before it replaces the current pull-and-reconcile catch-up step.

SparkFlow suits plants already running SparkBridge that are hitting the ceiling of tag-per-point scale, or that need a replayable application-tier backbone alongside Sparkplug rather than in place of it. It is commissioning today: live on the dev estate, proven against a licensed gateway, not yet released for purchase.
