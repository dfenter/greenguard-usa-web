Keep the equipment. Lose the middleman.

These drivers connect your Ignition gateway straight to older PLC racks, speaking their own protocols natively. The translator hardware in between comes out, along with its license, its patching and its failure modes.

That matters for accuracy as much as cost. Readings arrive with the equipment's own timing and its own fault state, instead of whatever a middle box was still serving from its last successful poll.

The line covers Siemens and CTI 505 series racks and IEC 104 utility equipment today, with the same approach and the same discipline behind each one.

Every driver in the line is proven against real equipment or the industry's own reference implementation before it goes near a customer site, and each one arrives with the evidence rather than the claim.

The result is fewer moving parts in the path from your plant to your data, and one fewer vendor to call when something in that path stops working.

If you have older racks still doing useful work and a growing need to get data off them, keeping the equipment and removing the middleman is usually the cheaper end of that decision.
