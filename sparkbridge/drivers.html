The line

Two protocols, two states

They share a build, a test discipline and a diagnostics surface. They do not share a stage of readiness, and the drawing on the lineup page says so.

TI 505 · CTI 2500

CAMP over TCP, with a real browse

The Ethernet encapsulation of the 505 task codes, spoken natively on port 1505. Browse returns the memory areas a 505 engineer expects, paged into sub-ranges when an area is large, and any address can be added on demand rather than declared in advance. Installed and running on a gateway today.

IEC 60870-5-104

The utility client, session and all

Interrogation, spontaneous reporting, clock synchronisation and the full APCI session lifecycle, proven across a 70-second capture against the lib60870 reference server with zero sequence faults. It has a page of its own.

Why native

What a translator costs you

The case for a driver rather than a bridge is not performance. It is fidelity.

What you keep

  • The device’s own timestamp, rather than the moment a middle box happened to poll.
  • The device’s own fault state, rather than a stale value a translator kept serving.
  • One less machine to license, patch, monitor and restart at two in the morning.

How they are built

  • The protocol core of each driver is plain Java with no gateway dependencies, so it can be tested against a simulator rather than a plant.
  • Addresses are parsed on demand, so a tag can be added without redeclaring the device.
  • Every driver is expected to arrive with its capture, not just its claim.
