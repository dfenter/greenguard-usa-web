Keep the equipment. Lose the middleman.

Every driver in this line is proven against real equipment or the industry's own reference implementation before it goes near a customer site, and each one arrives with the evidence rather than the claim. The translator hardware in between comes out, along with its license, its patching and its failure modes.

That matters for accuracy as much as cost. Readings arrive with the equipment's own timing and its own fault state, instead of whatever a middle box was still serving from its last successful poll. The result is fewer moving parts between your plant and your data, and one fewer vendor to call when something in that path stops working.

Four Situations This Solves

In each one the equipment is fine. The thing in front of it is the problem.

The situationWhat changes
An Allen-Bradley controller reaches Ignition through an OPC server for no reason but habit
Any plant running ControlLogix or CompactLogix through a middle layer.		 SparkLogix talks EtherNet/IP straight to the controller from the Ignition gateway and browses its tags directly, nested user-defined types included. The OPC server and its own point of failure come out of the path.
A Siemens or CTI rack that works fine is trapped behind a translator
A plant with 505 series racks still doing useful work.		 The rack is reliable, but everything you want from it arrives through a box with its own license, its own patching and its own way of failing. The TI 505 / CTI 2500 driver speaks CAMP over TCP straight from the Ignition gateway and browses the memory areas directly, V, WX, WY, X, Y, C, K, STW, TCP and TCC. The translator comes out and the rack stays where it is.
DNP3 equipment that needs a gateway box in between
The North American utilities that run DNP3 as a matter of course.		 DNP3 is standard equipment across the industry, and connecting it usually still means per site hardware to buy, rack and maintain. The DNP3 driver removes that box the same way the others do, running on the gateway you already have. Each driver in the line is proven against real equipment or the industry's own reference implementation before it goes near a customer site.
Someone has quoted a rip and replace
The manager weighing a capital project against a data problem.		 Replacing working racks is being proposed to solve a connectivity problem, not an equipment problem, and it is the expensive end of that decision. Keeping the equipment and removing the middleman costs $495 per gateway for the driver, bought once and kept, with no SparkBridge license required. That is usually a smaller number than the boxes it replaces, before the capital project is even discussed.
Readings look stale and nobody can prove where they came from
Anyone trying to trust a trend built from legacy plant.		 A middle box can keep serving its last successful poll, so the data looks live and the fault state looks clean when neither is true. Reading natively means values arrive with the equipment's own timing and its own fault state. The drivers read only and cannot issue a control command, so nothing in this path can operate equipment.

If you have older racks still doing useful work and a growing need to get data off them, keeping the equipment and removing the middleman is usually the cheaper end of that decision.
