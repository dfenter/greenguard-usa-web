SparkBridge Edge

The gateway at your site that never loses a reading

Edge is the Ignition module installed at each site. It watches the tags you choose, sends each value once when it changes, and carries the time your equipment gave it. Every link goes down sometime. Edge holds your plant's data when that happens, and gets it moving again the moment the link is back, with nothing lost and nothing sent twice.

The link drops. Edge keeps recording to disk. Every reading arrives on its real time once the link is back.

Each value leaves the site once, on the timestamp your equipment gave it.

Most sites poll. Something in the middle asks the equipment for every value, over and over, whether or not anything happened. That costs more every time you add a tag or a dashboard, and it has no good answer for a dropped link.

Edge sends a value once, when it changes, stamped with the time your equipment gave it. If the link goes down, Edge keeps recording to a local buffer on disk. When the link comes back, the backlog arrives in order and lands on the time it actually happened, not the time it reached the server.

You point Edge at a folder of tags on the site gateway. It reads the structure of that folder, including UDTs, and announces it to Central the moment it connects, so Central builds the matching tags without a mapping file. Folders at a depth you choose become devices with their own announcements, so a new pump or a renamed skid appears at Central on its own. Units, descriptions and ranges travel with that announcement instead of with every value.

Edge watches for the primary host. While Central is away it buffers, and when Central returns it replays in order. One site gateway can run up to eight independent edge nodes, each with its own connection, tag set and buffer, so one problem stays in one place. Write-back is off by default and is allowed only for the devices and points you list.

Edge publishes to Provider or to any conformant MQTT broker you already run, such as HiveMQ or Mosquitto, with TLS and per-site credentials. It carries its own license and works on its own or with Central.

Nothing Is Lost During an Outage

A dropped link is routine at a site on cellular or radio. GreenGuard USA built Edge around that fact instead of hoping it never happens.

Without it

The trend goes flat, then jumps

Most systems record when a value arrives. An outage leaves a hole, and the report built on that stretch of data is wrong. Somebody has to notice, explain it, and hope nobody downstream relied on it.

With Edge

The backlog fills in where it belongs

Edge keeps recording locally while the link is down. On reconnect, every reading arrives stamped with the time your equipment gave it, so the trend your team sees matches what the plant actually ran.

What SparkLink Saves You on a Metered Link

Comes with Edge, no extra charge. For a site on cellular or satellite, it keeps the monthly bill predictable.

A remote pad, a lift station or a tank farm on a metered link pays for data by the byte. Today the only controls most sites have are settings picked by hand at commissioning: no visibility into what a site actually spends and no way to see which tag drives the cost.

SparkLink measures what every site spends and attributes it down to the individual tag. If spend runs ahead of plan, it eases off gradually rather than all at once. Anything you mark safety-relevant is never touched, at any spend level. You get a monthly answer to "what did this site cost," and a site that stays inside its plan without you watching it.

For Your Engineers

Edge is a Sparkplug B edge node module for Ignition 8.1.19 and newer. It publishes report-by-exception with a durable store-and-forward buffer on disk, so a broken MQTT session cannot drop data. SparkLink shapes deadbands and publish intervals against a measured byte budget, with a fixed set of metrics exempt from any degradation. Verified against the Eclipse Sparkplug TCK, zero FAIL lines across ten runs. Detail on the technology page.
