The Barton Creek Factor

Why Live Oak Canopy Creates Extreme Pressure

Westlake Hills sits within the Barton Creek watershed, where mature live oak canopy does something that most homeowners do not expect: it concentrates mosquito habitat. Live oak leaf litter collects in the pockets and folds of the landscape, staying moist long after rainfall has dried from open surfaces. Tiger mosquitoes use this microhabitat for both daytime resting and container breeding in the natural debris cups that form at the base of mature trees.

The Barton Creek Greenbelt forms a continuous corridor of shaded, moist vegetation that runs directly adjacent to some of Westlake Hills most desirable residential addresses. Properties backing to the greenbelt face a sustained source of mosquito pressure that originates in habitat that cannot be treated. The greenbelt replenishes the local population regardless of what yard-level treatment is applied.

Effective protection for Westlake Hills properties requires interception, not just yard-level treatment. A CO₂ trap positioned at the lawn-to-treeline transition intercepts tiger mosquitoes on their approach from the greenbelt before they reach your patio or pool. Our placement guide for Barton Creek corridor properties covers the specific positioning that produces the best results for this terrain.
