How GreenGuard traps actually work

Every GreenGuard trap runs on one idea: counterflow. We put out a lure that smells exactly like prey — CO₂, heat, sometimes scent — and we run a capture airflow that opposes the path a mosquito takes toward that lure. The bug flies upstream toward the “breath,” crosses into a faster airstream it can’t fight, and is pulled into the trap. Below: the principle, then each trap in cross-section.

The counterflow principle

A mosquito can fly maybe 1–4 mph. If the lure plume drifts one way and the intake pulls air the other way faster than she can fly, then the moment she reaches the source she’s already in the intake. That’s the whole trick — and it’s why these beat sticky traps and zappers, which just wait to be bumped into.

Monolith family

Slat downdraft counterflow

The flagship. A tall column with a vented slat band around its waist, a powerful fan inside, and a catch tube at the bottom. Monolith, Mini, Black Fly and Titan are all the same machine at different scale.

  1. 1
    CO₂ curtain falls

    A ring emitter under the roof eave releases CO₂ + heat. CO₂ is ~1.5× denser than air, so the cool lure sinks in a curtain down the outside of the slats — exactly mimicking the plume a sleeping human gives off.

  2. 2
    Mosquitoes home in

    Female mosquitoes track the CO₂ gradient upwind toward the source. The curtain leads them straight onto the slat band.

  3. 3
    Fan pulls them through

    The Fantech FG10 draws air inward through the 8 mm slats at ~3.4 mph — faster than a mosquito can fly against. Once they cross the slats they're in the airstream and cannot escape.

  4. 4
    Caught & dried

    Air + bugs drop into the 10 in catch tube. The mosquitoes dehydrate and die; the air exhausts out the bottom.

Aria

Hidden-tank, face-down counterflow

The luxury line. Everything — fan, plenum, catch cup and the 20 lb CO₂ cylinder — hides inside a decorative planter, lantern or urn. The engine faces DOWN: it pulls air down a central throat while the CO₂ curtain projects out the top louvers.

  1. 1
    CO₂ out the top louvers

    A sealed annular plenum carries CO₂ + scent up and out a ring of louvers at the rim, forming a curtain that drifts outward.

  2. 2
    Mosquitoes ride the curtain

    Bugs follow the curtain back toward the rim, right over the open central throat.

  3. 3
    Fan sucks them straight down

    A 92 mm axial fan at the top of the throat pulls the bugs down the center, against the rising curtain — true counterflow.

  4. 4
    Split exhaust

    A baffle splits the fan's output: ~30% feeds the CO₂ curtain back up top, ~70% exhausts quietly out base vents. Bugs land in a 150 mm clear catch cup just under the fan.

Halo Garden

Face-up counterflow

A garden sculpture (lotus, agave, cairn, birdbath…) that hides the tank in its core. The engine faces UP: an annular grille on top, fan just beneath, catch cup below that. It works like a reverse chimney.

  1. 1
    Plume rises from the grille

    CO₂ + heat vents up through the center of the annular grille and rises in a warm plume above the trap.

  2. 2
    Mosquitoes descend onto it

    Bugs tracking the plume come DOWN toward the grille from above — the opposite of Halo's downward-facing cousins.

  3. 3
    Sucked down through the grille

    The 92 mm fan just below the grille pulls air down through the 4.2–5.2 in annular slot. Descending mosquitoes get drawn straight through.

  4. 4
    Collected below

    They drop into the catch cup beneath the fan; the 20 lb CO₂ cylinder sits hidden in the core column below.

Eave

Shade-harborage updraft

Mosquitoes spend the daytime hiding in cool, shaded shelter. Eave fakes the perfect one — an overhanging ledge with a dark cavity beneath — then vacuums the resters straight up into a bag.

  1. 1
    A patch of cool shade

    The overhanging ledge keeps a dark, humid cavity beneath the box. By day, mosquitoes hunt for exactly this kind of sheltered harborage to rest out of the sun.

  2. 2
    CO₂ settles into it

    CO₂ vented from the hidden tank is heavier than air, so it sinks and pools in the shaded cavity — making the resting spot smell alive and irresistible.

  3. 3
    Floor fan pulls an updraft

    A fan set in the floor of the box draws air straight up out of the cavity through an intake throat — faster than a resting mosquito can react.

  4. 4
    Lifted into the bag

    The resters are sucked up the duct into a mesh trap bag in the box, where they're held and dehydrate. Empty the bag to service.

Spire

Solar-chimney updraft

An obelisk with a sun-warmed black skin and a hollow flue. No fan, no power — the sun does the work.

  1. 1
    Sun heats the stack

    The black surface warms the air in the hollow flue; hot air rises and pulls a steady updraft up the column.

  2. 2
    CO₂ lures bugs in low

    A CO₂ lip around the base draws mosquitoes to the intake at ground level.

  3. 3
    Updraft carries them up

    The rising air sweeps them up the flue into a retention screen at the top where they can't fly back down.

Reed

CO₂-jet Venturi ejector

A cattail cluster that runs on gas pressure alone. A fast CO₂ jet does the pumping — no electricity.

  1. 1
    CO₂ jet fires up the throat

    A nozzle shoots CO₂ at high speed through a pinched venturi throat.

  2. 2
    Venturi suction at the side

    Fast jet = low pressure. That suction pulls surrounding air — and any mosquito near it — in through a side port.

  3. 3
    Swept up to the screen

    Entrained bugs ride the jet up the diffuser into a capture screen at the top.

Bloom

Electrostatic adhesive petals

An oversized flower. The petals are the trap; service is just swapping cards.

  1. 1
    Lit like a real bloom

    CO₂, heat and a UV glow at the center mimic a nectar source.

  2. 2
    Bugs land on the petals

    Mosquitoes settle on the petal faces, drawn to the center.

  3. 3
    Stuck on contact

    Each petal is a charged adhesive card; landing bugs stick fast and are removed when the cards are swapped.

  2. 2

  3. 3
    Larvae killed too

    Any eggs that hatch can't survive the treated water, breaking the breeding cycle.
