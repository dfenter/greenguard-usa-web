Decoupled curtain design: a fan-speed sweep showed the original coupled engine loses capture as the fan ramps (the louver jet flings the lure outward). Fix: vent the fan exhaust low through a hidden base ring so the top stays pure suction, and project the CO₂ curtain outward at a velocity matched to the suction. That holds full coverage and capture at 6 m/s, where the old design collapsed to 0.2%. *Absolute capture is model-optimistic; the robust result is the architecture and the balance rule.

Test 1 — Airflow & CO₂ plume

Counterflow field ✓ pass

Potential-flow solve on the engine: central throat = suction (sink), fan exhaust vented low at a hidden base ring, CO₂ curtain projected outward from the top ring. At the 6 m/s / ~43 CFM operating point the lure cloud covers 100% of the approach zone and the suction stays concentrated at the throat.

Test 2 — Smoke / mosquito capture

Throat capture ✓ near-total of approachers*

1,200 mosquito-proxy parcels seeded in the approach torus, flown as host-seekers tracking the CO₂ gradient toward the source. With the decoupled curtain holding the lure over the unit and the cranked suction pulling down the throat, nearly all approaching insects (blue) are drawn in. *The absolute figure is model-optimistic; what the test establishes is that the decoupled architecture removes the coverage-vs-capture tradeoff that capped the old design at ~27%.

Test 3 — Wind stability (per form)

Tip-over ✓ all forms pass 50 mph

Drag vs. resisting moment with the hidden tank as low ballast; safety factor ≥1.5 required at a 50 mph storm gust. The two tall forms were widened (cast feet) after the first run to clear the bar.
