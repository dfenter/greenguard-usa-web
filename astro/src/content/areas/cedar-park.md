Cedar Park's mosquito pressure is concentrated along Brushy Creek but extends into every established neighborhood through container breeding. The Asian tiger mosquito (*Aedes albopictus*) is the dominant problem species — it breeds in as little as a bottle cap of water, bites during daylight hours, and is completely missed by dusk-targeted spray programs.

## Cedar Park Mosquito Hotspots

**Brushy Creek Regional Trail corridor** from Lakeline Park to Twin Creeks sees the highest sustained pressure in Cedar Park. Shaded trail edges, creek overflow zones, and adjacent residential lots all contribute.

**Twin Creeks and Cypress Canyon** neighborhoods experience elevated pressure from the tree canopy and creek-adjacent lots that retain moisture well past rain events.

**Buttercup Creek** has older landscape infrastructure with mature trees and established irrigation — prime tiger mosquito habitat.

**Cedar Park Town Center area** retention ponds along Whitestone and 183A support large standing populations during warm months.

## Cedar Park Mosquito Season

Cedar Park's mosquito season mirrors Austin's: approximately March through November, with peak pressure May through October. Williamson County's slightly higher elevation does not meaningfully reduce season length compared to Travis County.
