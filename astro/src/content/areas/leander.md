Leander's growth pace — it added more residents per year than almost any city in Texas during the 2010s and 2020s — means its mosquito pressure is also growing. New development creates new retention ponds, new irrigation, and new container breeding habitat every year. Professional CO2 trapping scales with that growth in a way that homeowner-level treatments cannot.

## Leander Mosquito Hotspots

**Crystal Falls** community has the elevation changes, creek-adjacent lots, and extensive common area green space that creates sustained multi-source pressure.

**Bryson** newer development has multiple retention ponds and the amenity center infrastructure common to master-planned communities.

**Travisso** hilltop community experiences less natural breeding pressure but irrigation and landscaping in the development creates suburban tiger mosquito habitat.

**Mason Hills and Summerlyn** neighborhoods have older development with established vegetation and HOA features that sustain pressure through dry periods.

## Leander Mosquito Season

Season: April through October. Williamson County's slightly drier climate means a slightly shorter peak season than Travis County lakefront properties, but pressure during core months (June through September) is equivalent.
