Buda is one of the Austin metro's fastest-growing suburbs, and its mosquito profile is evolving rapidly. What was once a quiet small town with limited suburban breeding infrastructure is now a city of dense neighborhoods, HOA retention ponds, and irrigated common areas — all of which amplify the natural pressure from Onion Creek and the surrounding watershed.

## Buda Mosquito Hotspots

**Onion Creek corridor** provides the most consistent natural pressure in Buda. Creek-adjacent properties in Garlic Creek and Whispering Hollow experience Culex activity throughout warm months combined with container-breeding tiger mosquito pressure from suburban landscaping.

**Sunfield** one of Hays County's largest master-planned developments, has extensive retention pond infrastructure, a large amenity lake, and irrigated common areas that sustain pressure independent of rainfall.

**Garlic Creek** and older established Buda neighborhoods have the mature vegetation and landscape infrastructure that creates sustained tiger mosquito colonies throughout the season.

**Main Street corridor and Historic Buda** older commercial and residential areas have established vegetation and drainage features that concentrate breeding in the center of the city.

## Buda Mosquito Season

Season: April through October. Hays County's position slightly south of Austin actually extends peak season pressure marginally. The Onion Creek watershed retains moisture effectively and sustains breeding through dry periods.
