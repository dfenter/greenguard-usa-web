Liberty Hill sits at the frontier of Austin's growth, transitioning from rural Williamson County into a suburban city. That transition brings both the natural mosquito pressure of the Hill Country and the infrastructure-driven pressure of suburban development — a combination that older or newer residents may not have anticipated.

## Liberty Hill Mosquito Hotspots

**San Gabriel River corridor** properties face the highest natural pressure in Liberty Hill. River-adjacent lots experience Culex species from the river combined with container-breeding tiger mosquitoes from the surrounding landscape.

**Santa Rita Ranch** one of Williamson County's largest master-planned communities, has the amenity center, retention ponds, and irrigated common areas that concentrate breeding pressure throughout the development.

**Stonewall Ranch and Bar W Ranch** rural-residential communities with acreage lots accumulate standing water across larger footprints than suburban properties, sustaining pressure through dry periods.

**Highway 29 corridor** newer commercial and residential development is adding suburban retention infrastructure to previously rural land, creating new pressure zones each year.

## Liberty Hill Mosquito Season

Season: April through October. Rural Hill Country properties have slightly shorter peak seasons than Austin proper, but acreage properties can sustain stock tank and low-spot breeding well into November.
