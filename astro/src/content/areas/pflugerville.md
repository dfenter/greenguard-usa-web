Pflugerville's combination of a large lake, active creek system, and dense suburban development makes it one of the more complex mosquito environments in the northeast Austin metro. The two-species problem here — lake-sourced Culex at dusk and neighborhood tiger mosquitoes during the day — requires a comprehensive trapping strategy.

## Pflugerville Mosquito Hotspots

**Lake Pflugerville shoreline and park areas** provide the highest Culex pressure in the city. Evening activities near the lake can be severely impacted during peak season without professional control.

**Gilleland Creek corridor** runs through older Pflugerville neighborhoods and provides continuous natural breeding habitat from spring through fall.

**Spring Trails and Wildhorse** newer developments have retention infrastructure that sustains pressure during dry periods when natural sites diminish.

**Highland Park** older neighborhood with mature trees and established irrigation creates classic tiger mosquito habitat throughout the subdivision.

## Pflugerville Mosquito Season

Season: April through October, with occasional early-season pressure in March during warm years. The city's flat terrain extends post-rain breeding windows compared to hilly Travis County locations.
