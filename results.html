The Science Behind It

Why CO2 trapping works

The effectiveness of CO2 mosquito trapping is grounded in fundamental mosquito biology and documented in peer-reviewed research spanning over 16 years.

CO2 is the primary host-finding signal

Mosquitoes locate blood hosts primarily by detecting carbon dioxide exhaled during breathing. CO2 triggers a host-seeking behavioral response at concentrations as low as a few parts per million above ambient levels. A CO2 trap exploits this behavior by presenting a continuous, consistent CO2 plume that is indistinguishable from a resting host at distances of up to 100 feet.

Source: Dekker, T. et al. Wageningen University mosquito olfaction research

Targeting breeding females removes the source

Only female mosquitoes seek blood meals -- they require the protein to develop eggs. By targeting blood-seeking females, CO2 traps disproportionately remove the reproducing segment of the population. Each female captured represents dozens of potential offspring that will not be produced. Over 2 to 4 weeks, this disrupts the local breeding cycle in a way that pesticide sprays -- which kill adults indiscriminately and temporarily -- cannot replicate.

Source: Biogents field efficacy studies, peer-reviewed 2003-2019

BG-Sweetscent enhances species coverage

The Biogents BG-Mosquitaire uses the proprietary BG-Sweetscent lure in combination with CO2. This synthetic blend mimics human skin odor compounds and has been demonstrated to significantly increase catch rates for Aedes mosquitoes -- the primary vector of Zika, dengue, and chikungunya -- which respond less strongly to CO2 alone. The combination captures a broader range of mosquito species than CO2-only systems.

Source: Biogents AG, proprietary lure validation studies

The Mosqitter Grand 4-way attraction system

The Mosqitter Grand combines CO2, radiant heat (mimicking body temperature), UV light, and the Mosqilure scent attractant to create a multi-signal host simulation. Field studies show that multi-signal traps capture a broader spectrum of species and achieve higher total catch rates than single-signal CO2 traps, making the Grand the preferred system for larger properties with diverse mosquito species pressure.

Source: Mosqitter GmbH field validation documentation

Population-level suppression vs temporary knockdown

Pesticide spray programs achieve temporary relief by killing adult mosquitoes present at the time of treatment. Because they do not address the breeding population, adults from surrounding areas repopulate the treated zone within days. CO2 trapping creates cumulative suppression -- each week of operation removes additional breeding females, compounding the effect over time and delivering results that improve rather than reset every few weeks.

Source: Comparative efficacy literature, CDC vector control guidelines

Independent validation and public health use

The Biogents BG-Mosquitaire has been validated in independent field studies conducted by universities, public health institutions, and research agencies across Europe, Southeast Asia, and the Americas. It is used by public health departments for vector surveillance and control programs. The Mosqitter Grand was deployed at the G20 Summit in 2023 for perimeter mosquito control at the event venue.

Source: Published field efficacy studies, G20 event documentation
