What Westlake Hills Homeowners Are Doing Differently This Year

Westlake Hills consistently ranks among the most desirable residential communities in Austin. The live oak canopy, the topography, the proximity to Barton Creek, and the scale of the properties are all part of what makes it exceptional. These same features create mosquito conditions that are more complex than most of the city.

GreenGuard USA  ·  April 17, 2025

What the Live Oak Canopy Does

Live oak is Austin's defining tree, and Westlake Hills has it in abundance. It is also an ideal mosquito environment. The canopy provides shade that prevents soil drying. Leaf litter accumulates in the pockets and crevices of mature oaks, holding moisture and creating the kind of microhabitat where tiger mosquitoes prefer to rest during the day.

Tiger mosquitoes, Aedes aegypti, are the primary biting pressure in Westlake Hills residential areas. They rarely travel more than two hundred feet from their breeding site, which means the pressure in any given backyard is almost entirely local in origin, and almost entirely originating from the property itself or the adjacent greenbelt.

Why Conventional Spray Programs Have Limitations Here

Spray programs work by applying pesticide to vegetation and surfaces. They are effective at killing what is present on the property at the time of application. What they do not address is the breeding habitat in the leaf litter, the shaded organic matter in drainage channels, and the greenbelt vegetation that cannot be treated.

In Westlake Hills, where properties are often large and the greenbelt boundary is close, the source of mosquito pressure is frequently in places that a spray program cannot reach. This is one reason many Westlake Hills homeowners have moved toward CO₂ trapping as a primary strategy. It does not require treating the source. It works by intercepting adults before they reach the living areas, continuously, across the full season.

The Pollinator Garden Consideration

Westlake Hills has a high concentration of native plant gardens, certified wildlife habitats, and homeowners who maintain pollinators deliberately. This population has been particularly resistant to spray programs because of the well-documented harm pyrethroids cause to bees and beneficial insects.

CO₂ trapping is entirely compatible with native plant gardens and pollinator habitats. The trap targets only blood-seeking female mosquitoes. Bees and butterflies do not respond to the CO₂ and human scent signals that the trap uses, and nothing is released into the garden environment. This is covered in depth in our guide on mosquito control for pollinator gardens.
