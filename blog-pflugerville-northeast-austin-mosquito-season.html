Pflugerville and Northeast Austin: Mosquito Season Guide

Pflugerville and the northeast Austin corridor have grown rapidly over the past decade, and their mosquito season reflects that growth. Lake Pflugerville, Gilleland Creek, and the dense residential development pattern of the area combine to create sustained pressure that residents often find more intense than they expected.

GreenGuard USA  ·  May 28, 2025

Lake Pflugerville and Gilleland Creek

Lake Pflugerville is a popular community resource and a significant Culex mosquito breeding source. The slow-moving water in its coves and drainage channels provides ideal conditions for the southern house mosquito, which can travel up to two miles from its breeding site. Properties within a half-mile of Lake Pflugerville experience pressure that originates at the lake regardless of how well-maintained their own yard may be.

Gilleland Creek runs through much of established Pflugerville before connecting to the Colorado River system. Its banks provide additional breeding habitat that contributes to mosquito pressure in neighborhoods adjacent to its path. The combination of lake and creek pressure makes Pflugerville one of the higher-pressure communities in the northeast Austin corridor. Our Pflugerville service coverage is designed around these specific conditions.

The Suburban Development Factor

Pflugerville's rapid growth has added dense residential development that brings its own breeding habitat. Subdivision retention ponds, irrigation systems, and the container breeding habitat common to newer construction all compound the natural water feature pressure. The result is that even neighborhoods that are not immediately adjacent to Lake Pflugerville or Gilleland Creek face meaningful mosquito pressure from within their own built environment.

Newer subdivisions in Pflugerville often have more retention pond infrastructure than older ones, which creates higher baseline pressure in areas that residents may assume would be lower-pressure because the properties are newer.

Getting Ahead of the Season

The most effective mosquito control in Pflugerville and northeast Austin starts in March, before peak pressure builds. A CO₂ trap started early in the season builds population suppression during the low-pressure period when it is most efficient, and the protection is well-established by the time summer pressure arrives from Lake Pflugerville and Gilleland Creek.

For properties near the lake or creek, the Mosqitter Grand's two-acre coverage radius and four-signal attraction system provides meaningful interception of the off-site pressure that a single Biogents unit may not fully address.
