The Impossible Tradeoff, Until Now

If you maintain a pollinator garden in Austin, you have faced a problem that has no good solution with conventional mosquito control: every effective spray product is also toxic to the bees and beneficial insects your garden depends on. Native bees, honeybees, butterflies, and hoverflies are non-target casualties of any broad-spectrum insecticide application, regardless of how carefully it is applied.

Organic gardeners, certified wildlife habitats, and native plant enthusiasts have largely been forced to choose between mosquito protection and a functioning garden ecosystem. CO₂ trapping resolves this conflict completely.

CO₂ traps are attracted only by the CO₂ signal released by warm-blooded hosts. Bees, butterflies, and beneficial insects do not produce this signal and are not affected by the trap in any way.

Why Bees Are Completely Safe

The mechanism of CO₂ trapping is specific at the biological level. Female mosquitoes initiate host-seeking behavior in response to rising CO₂ concentrations, a signal produced by mammals and birds exhaling. Bees, butterflies, and other garden insects do not generate this signal and do not respond to it.

The scent attractants used with CO₂ traps, human skin compounds like lactic acid, ammonia, and octenol in the Biogents BG-Sweetscent, or Mosqilure in the Mosqitter Grand, are equally specific to blood-seeking insects. These compounds do not attract bees, which use entirely different olfactory cues (floral volatiles, pheromones) to navigate their environment.

Unlike sprays, which coat foliage and persist in the environment, CO₂ traps release only carbon dioxide, a gas present in the atmosphere at 0.04% and posing no risk to any plant, insect, or organism in the garden.

Placement in Pollinator Gardens

For maximum effectiveness in garden settings, trap placement requires some thought:

Position upwind of breeding areas, not in the garden bed itself. Place the trap at the edge of your lawn, between the mosquito breeding habitat (shaded moist areas, standing water sources) and your seating area. The CO₂ plume should travel toward the breeding zone, not across your flower beds.

Keep at least 10 feet from pollinator-attracting flowering plants. Not because the trap harms bees, but because bees actively foraging in flowers could be drawn into the intake airflow at close range. This is a precautionary spacing, not a safety requirement.

Run continuously. Population suppression requires 24/7 operation. Switching the trap off when bees are foraging is unnecessary and counterproductive, bees are not affected by the trap regardless of timing.

Integrating Mosquito Control With Organic Certification

CO₂ mosquito trapping is fully compatible with organic garden certification. No pesticides, herbicides, or synthetic compounds are applied to your soil, plants, water features, or any surface in your garden. The trap uses CO₂ (a naturally occurring atmospheric gas) and physical capture, there is nothing to conflict with organic standards or pollinator-friendly certifications.

For Austin gardeners maintaining National Wildlife Federation Certified Wildlife Habitats, Monarch Waystation certifications, or Texas Superstar programs, CO₂ trapping is the only mosquito control approach that does not require any exemptions or qualification review.
